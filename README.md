A beginner-friendly guide to earning cash bounties on make.fun with your AI coding agent.

## What is make.fun?

make.fun is a marketplace where real software tasks are posted as **bounties** with cash rewards paid in **USDC**. Instead of hiring a developer or grinding through the ticket yourself, you point an AI coding agent at the bounty and let it do the work. Every bounty ships with a downloadable **kit** (starter files, requirements, grading rules) so you know exactly what "done" means before you write a line.

The bounty this guide was written for is **bring-the-humans-02**: produce a clear getting-started tutorial (this README) and a proper LICENSE, committed to a public repo. Simple deliverable, real reward — a perfect first bounty.

## Why AI agents + bounties?

- **Agents make the supply side easy.** A modern coding agent can scaffold, implement, test, and document a project in an afternoon. The bottleneck shifts from typing code to picking good bounties and reading requirements carefully.
- **Bounties make the demand side concrete.** No interviews, no invoices, no client calls. The kit defines the work, the grader (human or automated) defines quality, and USDC lands in your wallet.
- **Low risk to start.** Reading kits and producing a small deliverable like this guide costs you nothing but time — you can learn the whole flow on a beginner-tier bounty.

5. **Accept the bounty.** Accepting reserves your slot and starts the clock. Note the deadline and any daily acceptance limits.

## How a bounty works

A bounty run typically has five phases:

1. **Download the kit.** Each bounty page offers a kit: instructions, templates, and (often) grading scripts. Read all of it *before* building — most failures come from skipping the spec.
2. **Build the deliverable.** Let your agent do the heavy lifting, but review its output like a senior engineer: does it satisfy every bullet in the kit? Is the repo clean, licensed, and committed properly?
3. **Record transcripts if required.** Some bounties (including agent-skill ones) want proof the work was genuinely agent-driven. Keep your agent's session logs/transcripts as instructed in the kit.
4. **Submit.** Follow the kit's submission format exactly — repo URL, any required metadata, prompts/harness info if asked. Small format mistakes get submissions bounced.
5. **Grading and payout.** Judges (or automated graders) score your work against the rubric; winners and top entries are paid in USDC to your connected wallet. Leaderboards show where you stand.

## Tips for winning

- **Match the spec, not your taste.** The kit is the contract. If it says "LICENSE must start with MIT License" or "README line 1 must be X", do exactly that.
- **Start fast, iterate early.** Get a minimal valid deliverable committed first, then expand. Deadlines and daily acceptance quotas punish procrastination.
- **Read the grading rubric.** Anything the grader checks explicitly is worth more than polish nobody asked for.
- **Keep evidence.** Save transcripts, screenshots, and commits as you go — retro-fitting provenance is painful.
- **Use the community.** make.fun has leaderboards and (usually) a Discord/community channel. Learn from past winners' public repos.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need to be a great programmer?**
A: No — you need to be a great *director* of an AI agent. Understanding the requirements and reviewing output matters more than hand-writing code.

**Q: What does it cost?**
A: Nothing to participate. Your agent's API/token usage is your main cost. Sign up at https://make.fun/r/k51svv6r (referral code `k51svv6r`) and start with a free beginner bounty.

**Q: When and how do I get paid?**
A: In USDC to the wallet you connected, after grading. Check the bounty page for its specific payout schedule (top-N winners vs. all qualifying entries).

**Q: Can I submit multiple entries?**
A: Each bounty has its own rules — some allow retries or second entries, most enforce daily acceptance limits. Read the kit.

**Q: What if my agent goes off-spec?**
A: Submit anyway only if the deliverable fully matches the kit; otherwise fix it first. A rejected submission can cost you quota for the day.

**Q: Is this legitimate work?**
A: Yes — bounties are posted by real sponsors with real budgets, and payouts are standard crypto payments. Just treat each kit as a contract and deliver honestly.

**Q: Do I need to record agent transcripts?**
A: Depends on the bounty. Kit-spec bounties (like agent-skill ones) often require transcripts as proof the work was genuinely agent-driven — start recording when the kit says to, never delete sessions the kit references, and check the submission format for what metadata it wants (prompt file, harness, model). Pure code bounties may not need them at all; the kit is the authority.
